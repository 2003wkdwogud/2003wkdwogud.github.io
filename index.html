About

I am an Electrical Engineering undergraduate at the University at Buffalo and an undergraduate research assistant in the Media Forensics Lab (MDFL), working under Professor Siwei Lyu and Chengzhe Sun. I also serve as Audio Team Technical Leader for the Institute for Artificial Intelligence and Data Science (IAD) and MDFL.

My research asks how we can determine whether audio is authentic, localize manipulated segments, and reconstruct how a recording has been processed. I develop detection and sequence modeling methods and study their robustness across datasets, generators, and neural audio codecs.

Alongside my own research, I coordinate experiments and mentor graduate and undergraduate researchers working on biomedical audio analysis, accessible speech technology, and robust audio watermarking. My work includes experiment design, PyTorch-based audio pipelines, and model evaluation.

Selected Research

Cross-Codec Generalization

Cross-Codec Speech Deepfake Generalization

2026 – Present

Studying how detector training on one neural audio codec transfers to other codecs, and how those transfer relationships can guide training data selection.

  • Constructed a 15 × 15 cross-codec benchmark spanning 15 neural codecs and 210 directed transfer pairs.
  • Evaluated codec-level explanations for generalization and quantified pair-specific transfer behavior.
  • Developed a transfer-aware codec subset selection strategy and validated selected subsets through detector retraining.
15 neural codecs · 210 directed transfer pairs · Transfer-aware training data selection
Detection & Localization

Partial Deepfake Localization under Distribution Shift

2026 – Present

Investigating whether detectors can identify and temporally localize manipulated speech when evaluated beyond their training distribution.

  • Conduct cross-dataset evaluation across PartialSpoof, Half-Truth, and LlamaPartialSpoof.
  • Analyze source-dependent transfer failure, generator-specific behavior, and differences in manipulation structure.
  • Evaluate multi-source training and augmentation strategies for out-of-domain robustness.
Audio Provenance

Single-Recording Audio Provenance Reconstruction

2026 – Present

Reconstructing a recording’s ordered processing history from a single recording without a reference signal.

  • Formulated processing-history reconstruction as a sequence-decoding problem.
  • Combined frozen self-supervised speech encoders with an autoregressive Transformer decoder.
  • Outperformed matched flat and independent prediction baselines.
68.3% exact-chain accuracy in the evaluated setting
